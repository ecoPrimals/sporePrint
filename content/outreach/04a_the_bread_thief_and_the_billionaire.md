The full essay lives in the philosophy section: **[The Loaves and the Fishes — Discovery as Revealing What Already Exists](@/philosophy/the_loaves_and_the_fishes.md)**.

It opens with the bread thief — a man stealing bread to feed his family, and why classical libertarianism can see the act but not the preconditions. From there it traces the structural pattern through pollution as subsidy, neoliberalism as perversion of capitalism, a Deist's journey through world religions, and arrives at the loaves and the fishes reexamined: omniscience over omnipotence, revelation of what already exists over creation from nothing.

> *"The crowd was always fed. The provisions were always sufficient. The only thing missing was someone willing to give first."*

The essay connects to [detroit.primals.eco](https://detroit.primals.eco) through the [Two Eras of Machine Politics](https://detroit.primals.eco/analysis/historical-pattern/) — the bread thief problem applied to public education: when a convicted felon extracts $4.9M/year from children's education funding, the question is not just "is the theft wrong?" but "what preconditions allowed a school system to be structured so that theft was possible?"
