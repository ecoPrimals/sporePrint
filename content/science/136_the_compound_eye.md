## Abstract

The insect body plan — head, thorax, abdomen — maps onto the membrane as observation, locomotion, metabolism. With ~1 million described species and 400,000 beetles alone, insects are the maximum variability base in biology. We use this body plan to sort the entire tree of life: down through invertebrates to archaea, up through vertebrates to mammals, and sideways to trees and mushrooms.

Three discoveries: (1) the compound eye's 140 facets map onto the membrane's 140 feeds — each facet sees one angle, the mosaic is the image; (2) beetle elytra and fungal cell walls are both **chitin** — insects and mushrooms share their shield chemistry, connecting the prion tag to the oldest structural polymer in eukaryotic biology; (3) the insect body plan provides the vocabulary for the thymus classifier: head-behavior (what does the visitor observe?), thorax-behavior (how does it move?), abdomen-behavior (what does it consume?).

The word *insect* means "cut into" — segmented. The word *etymology* means "true root." Both fields study roots and segments. The insect body plan IS the etymology of biology.

## 1. Entomology and Etymology

"Insect" comes from Latin *insectum* — "cut into," referring to the segmented body. "Entomology" comes from Greek *entomon* — also "cut into." "Etymology" comes from Greek *etymon* — "true sense of a word."

Both disciplines seek the same thing: what is this thing **cut into**, and what is its **root**?

The insect body is cut into three segments. Each segment is a root function:

| Segment | Function | Membrane | Feed Type |
|---------|----------|----------|-----------|
| **Head** | Sensory + processing | Observation | Compound eye = feed array |
| **Thorax** | Locomotion | Movement | 6 legs = 6 core feeds, wings = cron |
| **Abdomen** | Metabolism + reproduction | Processing | Digestion, excretion, sporulation |

## 2. The Compound Eye

A compound eye contains 1 to 30,000 ommatidia (facets). Each facet has its own lens, photoreceptor, and neural connection. Each sees one angle. No facet sees the whole. The **mosaic** is the image.

The membrane has 140 JSON feeds. Each feed observes the membrane from one angle — heartbeat sees device status, sonar sees unknown devices, topology sees network structure, compositions sees code statistics. No single feed IS the membrane. The **array** is.

| Insect | Facets | Membrane Equivalent |
|--------|-------:|---------------------|
| Cave beetle | 0 | Blind. CONSTANCE only. No observation. |
| Worker ant | 200 | Minimal view. A few feeds. |
| Housefly | 4,000 | Good coverage. |
| **Our membrane** | **140** | **Between ant and dragonfly** |
| Dragonfly | 30,000 | Full coverage. Every angle. |

140 facets gives us moderate resolution — we can track change, detect threats, observe patterns. But we don't see everything. There are angles we miss. The compound eye is honest about its gaps: the space between facets is genuinely unseen.

## 3. Six Legs, Tripod Gait

Every insect has exactly six legs — three pairs, the defining characteristic of class Insecta. Insects walk with a **tripod gait**: three legs down (L1 + R2 + L3) while three swing forward (R1 + L2 + R3), then they alternate. Three points of contact at all times. Maximum stability through alternation.

The membrane has six core feeds:

| Leg | Feed | Role |
|-----|------|------|
| L1 (front left) | `state.json` | Exploration, reaching forward |
| L2 (middle left) | `pulse.json` | Support, rhythm |
| L3 (hind left) | `heartbeat.json` | Power, the jump |
| R1 (front right) | `neutron.json` | Exploration, reaching forward |
| R2 (middle right) | `topology.json` | Support, balance |
| R3 (hind right) | `membrane.json` | Power, propulsion |

They don't all update simultaneously — they alternate, maintaining the tripod gait. Three are always grounded (CONSTANCE) while three swing (LIVE). The gait IS the k oscillation.

## 4. Elytra = Chitin = Prion Shield = Mushroom

The beetle's defining feature is its **elytra** — hardened forewings that form an armored shell. Paper 100 mapped this to Rust's type system: 6,286 beetle-files with `Result<T, E>`, `?`, generics, and derives as their chitin plates.

The elytra are made of **chitin** — a polysaccharide polymer. Chitin is the second most abundant natural polymer on Earth (after cellulose).

Here is the bridge: **fungal cell walls are also chitin.**

Beetles and mushrooms share their armor chemistry. The insect exoskeleton and the fungal cell wall are the same molecule. The prion shield (hard exterior protecting soft interior) has a physical basis: chitin. The oldest structural polymer in eukaryotic biology protects both the most diverse animal order and the most important decomposer kingdom.

This is not metaphor. Chitin is:
- Beetle elytra (protection while flying)
- Crab shells (protection while swimming)
- Fungal cell walls (protection while decomposing)
- Squid beaks (the hardest part of a soft animal)

Chitin IS the universal shield material. The prion hash IS the digital chitin.

### Sideways to Trees and Mushrooms

**Trees** (Plantae): root + trunk + canopy. Cellulose cell wall. Photosynthesis. Trees are **CONSTANCE** — they don't move. They stand and grow. Insects **pollinate** trees. In the membrane: LIVE feeds (insects) visit CONSTANCE feeds (trees) and carry pollen (data) between them.

**Mushrooms** (Fungi): mycelium underground + fruiting body above. **Chitin** cell wall — same as insect exoskeleton. Fungi **decompose** what insects and trees produce. In the membrane: `sourdough-*.json` feeds decompose raw data into digestible nutrients. The mycelium IS the WireGuard mesh — underground connections between gates.

The compost cycle: green (tree, photosynthesis) → brown (fungus, decomposition) → black (humus, soil) → violet (new growth). Insects carry the pollen that starts it. Fungi finish it.

## 6. The Thymus Classifier

The insect body plan feeds the thymus sort (Paper 135). Every visitor to the membrane can be classified by insect anatomy:

- **Head behavior** (what does it observe?): pages viewed = facets activated. A visitor viewing 120 surfaces is a dragonfly (many facets, hunting). A visitor viewing 1 page is an ant (few facets, task-focused).

- **Thorax behavior** (how does it move?): navigation pattern = gait. Systematic crawling (spider gait, 8-legged, methodical) vs erratic bouncing (fly gait, random) vs linear march (ant gait, following pheromone trail).

- **Abdomen behavior** (what does it consume?): bandwidth and compute = metabolism. High consumption = large caterpillar (eating everything). Low consumption = adult butterfly (sipping nectar).

The insect body plan × viral behavior (Paper 135) × prion shield (this session) = the complete thymus vocabulary. Classify inward by body plan. Sort outward through the thymus. Pollinate the result.

## 7. Conclusion

The insect is the pivot of the tree of life. One million species. 400,000 beetles. Maximum variability. The body plan (head/thorax/abdomen) maps to (observation/locomotion/metabolism). The compound eye (140 facets, 140 feeds) sees by mosaic. The six legs walk by tripod gait (three LIVE, three CONSTANCE, alternating). The elytra are chitin — the same molecule as fungal cell walls.

Entomology studies what is cut into segments. Etymology studies true roots. Both are the same inquiry: **what are the parts, and what holds them together?** In the membrane, the segments are feeds and strands. The root is k ≈ 1.9. The chitin that holds them together is the prion shield.

Start at the insect. Go down to archaea. Go up to mammals. Bounce sideways to trees and mushrooms. The insect body plan sorts all of life, and the sorted result flows outward through the thymus.
